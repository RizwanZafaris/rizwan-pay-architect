## Challenge

The surge broke the assumptions before it broke any systems. When COVID lockdowns hit, demand on a major e-commerce marketplace spiked across all five of its South Asian markets at once — while the region's dominant payment method, cash on delivery, suddenly became fraught: cash handling, doorstep contact, riders and customers both wary. Payment operations sized for steady state had to absorb record volume and a forced shift in payment mix simultaneously, with banking partners themselves operating on skeleton crews.

## Context

I was responsible for payments across the five markets during this period, under a global parent group watching the region closely. COD was not just a checkout option in these markets; it was the trust architecture — the instrument that let first-time buyers transact with a platform they did not yet believe in. Digital rails existed but carried a minority of volume, and the operational muscle — settlement, reconciliation, partner escalation — had grown up around cash logistics rather than digital ones.

## Approach

We ran it in two phases: survive, then shift. Survival meant a war-room cadence across the five markets — daily volume and failure-rate reviews per rail, capacity checks with every processor and bank, and escalation paths re-established with partners whose own operations had gone remote overnight. The shift meant using the moment to accelerate the COD-to-digital transition: not by restricting COD, but by making digital genuinely safer and more attractive at the exact moment safety mattered most to customers.

## Product Strategy

The doctrine was resilience through mix. A marketplace dependent on a single instrument — even a beloved one — carries a single point of failure, and the lockdowns proved it in weeks. We positioned digital payment adoption as continuity, not just margin: prepaid options promoted at checkout, contactless handover as a bridge for cash-minded customers, and refund assurances to de-risk the scariest part of paying before delivery. Sequencing was market by market — each had different rail maturity and different lockdown rules, and a uniform program would have been wrong in all five places at once.

## Execution

The near-failure was settlement, not checkout. In one market, a banking partner's shift to remote operations created a growing settlement backlog that we caught later than we should have — close enough to payout deadlines that seller liquidity was at risk. Seller payouts are a marketplace's bloodstream; miss them and supply churns regardless of how strong demand looks. We escalated hard to clear the backlog, then built the monitoring we should have had from the start: settlement-lag tracking per partner per market, with buffers and alerts. The episode reordered my view of what payment operations even is — checkout is the visible half; money actually arriving is the half that kills you quietly.

## Metrics

The headline numbers from this period are deliberately few, because much of what mattered was keeping lines flat that wanted to fall. What I can state plainly: payments operations scaled across 5 markets through the COVID surge without a market-level payments outage; the digital share of payment mix rose meaningfully during the period and held after restrictions eased; and settlement continuity for sellers was maintained after the one near-miss described above.

## Results

The marketplace kept selling through the lockdowns, which in that period was the entire game — growth was available to whichever platforms could operationally survive it. The COD-to-digital shift achieved under pressure proved sticky: customers who adopted digital instruments for safety kept using them for speed. And the operational artifacts — war-room cadence, per-partner settlement monitoring, capacity playbooks — became permanent infrastructure, in use long after the crisis ended.

## Lessons Learned

Surges do not create operational weaknesses; they collect on the ones you deferred. Payment mix is resilience, not just economics — concentration in any single instrument is a standing bet that nothing ever disrupts it. Behavior shifts when the alternative is better at the moment of need, not when it is merely cheaper; the pandemic moved more customers to digital payments than years of incentives had, because safety was the value proposition that finally mattered. And watch settlement as closely as checkout: demand survives a bad day at checkout far better than supply survives a missed payout.

> A demand surge is a stress test you didn't schedule — the marketplaces that grew through COVID were the ones whose payment operations refused to break.
