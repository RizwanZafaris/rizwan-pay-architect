### First-90-minutes engagement protocol (non-negotiable)

1. Publish, then **stay on the platform 90 minutes**. Never post-and-ghost.
2. Reply to **every** comment with substance — add a fact, a number, or a question
   back. Minimum two sentences; no "Thanks!" replies.
3. For the 2–3 best comment threads, reply to the replies — depth of thread outranks
   count of comments in the feed algorithm.
4. Spend 15 of the 90 minutes commenting on 5+ adjacent posts from larger payments
   accounts (see daily list) — borrowed distribution while the post is warm.
5. If a recruiter, hiring manager, or target-company employee comments: substantive
   public reply first, then a short DM same day ("appreciated your point on X —
   what's your team seeing?"). Never pitch in the first DM.
6. No edits in the first hour (hygiene: draft is proofed before publishing, not after).

## 2. Hook System — 10 patterns fitted to his material

Rotation rule: never the same pattern twice in a row; max twice per week per pattern.
Hook = first 210 characters (what shows before "…see more"). Write the hook last,
make it carry a number or a tension, and never let line 1 be throat-clearing.

| # | Pattern | Formula | His example (ready to use) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Number-shock** | Improbable verified stat, then the turn | "270M+ payments a year at 97% success. The interesting part is the 3%." |
| 2 | **Myth-kill** | Quote the accepted belief, kill it with operator evidence | "Any PSP can claim 97% success. The question is — 97% of what? Publish the denominator or it's marketing." |
| 3 | **Interview-question** | The question he actually asks candidates; readers self-test | "I ask every payments PM candidate one question: 'Walk me through what happens after the user taps Pay.' Most answers stop three layers too early." |
| 4 | **Postmortem honesty** | Admit the expensive lesson first | "I ran a $3M transformation. The postmortem was harder to write than the business case." |
| 5 | **Frontier-vs-Stripe-world contrast** | Their default vs his reality | "Stripe-world: one API, one acquirer, done. My world: five frontier markets, carrier billing, wallets — and corridors where the settlement report is a file someone emails you." |
| 6 | **Tiny-detail-big-consequence** | Microscopic artifact, macroscopic outcome | "The most valuable string in payments isn't in your pitch deck. It's the name on the payment screen." |
| 7 | **Decision-framework** | The test he runs before a recurring decision | "T+1 or T+0? Before promising any merchant real-time settlement, I run a four-question test. Question one is about working capital, not technology." |
| 8 | **What-X-taught-me** | Unexpected teacher → transferable lesson | "Scaling an OTT platform from 0 to 5M subscribers taught me more about payments than any card program ever did." |
| 9 | **Contrarian-metric** | Demote the celebrated metric, promote his | "Stop celebrating payment success rate. Start measuring straight-through processing. 90% STP changed how this company sleeps; 97% success just decorated the deck." |
| 10 | **Story-cliffhanger** | True moment of tension, payoff below the fold | "The night a settlement file didn't arrive, I learned what a 99.95% SLA actually costs to keep." |

### 4.1 Payments — idea #2 · hook: number-shock · Pillar 1

We took payment cost from 50% to 1%.

The lesson isn't "carrier billing is expensive."

The lesson is that payment cost is a curve you
